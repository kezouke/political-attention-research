# Methods, scope and interpretation

## 1. Financial difficulty and climate concern

ESS8 edition 2.3, ESS10 edition 3.3, and ESS10SC edition 3.1 were downloaded from the official ESS portal on 30 October 2026. Intersect the countries in round 8 and round 10, giving 22 countries. ESS rounds are independent cross sections. Six round-10 countries use a separate self-completion file, whereas the round-10 interview file includes in-person and some video interviews.

Use `wrclmch` 1–5; set the outcome to 1 for 4–5, 0 for 1–3. For `hincfel`, combine values 1–2 (comfortable or coping) and 3–4 (difficult or very difficult). Exclude missing, refusal and don't-know responses. Apply `pspwght` within countries. Country group means give each country the same weight; sample sizes are unweighted valid response counts. The analysis is descriptive, not an estimate of actual income or poverty.

For the exploratory linear probability model, restrict to valid age (15–100), gender (1 or 2 as recorded), education (eisced 1–7), ideology (lrscale 0–10), and both outcome variables. Normalise weights to sum to 1 per country-wave. Regress climate concern on financial difficulty, round, their interaction, age in decades, gender, education, ideology and country indicators. Use country-clustered SE and t inference. Repeat for the 16 countries with round-10 interviews. Clustered variance does not fully incorporate ESS stratification, primary sampling units, design effects or mode changes; 16 or 22 clusters are limited. The analysis is observational, exploratory and not preregistered. Do not call coefficients causal effects or interpret a nonsignificant result as evidence of equality.

## 2. Party documents and the “80%” proposition

Manifesto Project 2023a is analysed on elections from 2010 to 2022 for nine comparator countries and Russia. Percentages are source-coded shares of quasi-sentences, not shares of voters or speeches. A document's total may contain additional unspecified or uncoded categories. Two definitions are proxies, not a complete interpretation of the original post:

- Narrow: all main economy categories `per401`–`per416`, plus anti-corruption `per304`.
- Broad: the above plus welfare `per503`/`per504`, labour `per701`/`per702`, democracy and constitutional categories `per202`–`per204`, and administration/institutions `per301`–`per303`/`per305`.

Each three-digit category is counted once. Broad political and welfare categories can include statements outside the claim. Conversely relevant ideas may be coded elsewhere, so neither definition is an upper bound for every conceivable reading. Each country-election value is the mean across usable documents weighted by `pervote`; for Brazil use `presvote`, the first-round presidential vote. We report the sum of vote shares covered and document count. This is conditional on available documents and may include electoral coalitions and substitute texts. It is not representative of missing parties. No interpolation between elections. The source is a 2023 edition and does not include Thailand or current elections for many countries.

## 3. Media and selective exposure

MeCCO monthly chart arrays were captured on 30 October 2026. Sum the published 12 monthly entries for each year; the year index treats the first published value as January of its stated start year. The three-country Russian basket omits Izvestiya, whose entire 2025 sequence is zero in the captured source. Divide each selected basket's annual count by its own 2010 count and multiply by 100. These are counts of keyword-matching articles in a small set of newspapers. They are not a share of all articles, reach, a random national media sample, or a personalised feed. Changes in indexing and output may matter.

CAP NYT Index is a systematic sample of index entries, specifically the first entry on odd-numbered pages. Count the primary topic label among nonmissing topic records by year for 2010–2016. The civil-rights category is broader than gender and identity. It cannot establish how identity topics compare in recent social feeds. An earlier TF–IDF/logistic classifier was tested on 2010–2016 with an earlier training window and was not used to infer trends beyond its test period because of poor recall for rare classes.

## 4. Economic issue priorities

SPES annual Eurobarometer aggregates provide country-year percentages naming each of several issues among the most important problems facing the country. The 14-country series is an unweighted mean of country percentages, not a population-weighted EU statistic. There is no 2011 observation; the chart breaks the line rather than interpolating. Multiple responses are allowed and shares cannot be summed. The harmonised environment category combines climate, environment and energy security.

The earlier model joins the country-year inflation series with price-mention share and compares country-fixed-effect specifications with and without year indicators. Standard errors are clustered by country. A sharp coefficient change when adding year indicators is a warning about common time shocks, not proof that inflation has no effect.

Read `data/raw/README.md` and the baseline source notes for dataset versions, original citations and access requirements. ## 5. Four-cell ESS table

`ess_four_groups.csv` gives all combinations of financial difficulty and severe climate worry for each matched country and wave. The denominator is everyone in that country-wave with valid answers to both questions and a positive post-stratification weight. Each cell includes its unweighted respondent count, the unweighted total, the weighted percentage and mode of the relevant file. The cell counts and percentages sum to the total and 100% within each country-wave. The ESS10 interview file includes some video interviews; the mode label is at the file level, not an individual interview-mode classification.

## 6. Independent text-analysis pilot on two Spanish programmes

The pilot uses the 2023 national election PDFs published by PP and PSOE. PyMuPDF 1.26.7 extracts text from page 9 onward for PP and page 11 onward for PSOE (one-based PDF page numbers), omitting initial contents pages. PDF layout can combine titles and distinct policy proposals. Text is split after sentence punctuation, then spans with fewer than eight word tokens are discarded. This is a mechanical unit, not a validated quasi-sentence or a representative sample of elections. Two parties in one country are not a proxy for six countries.

The dictionaries in `analysis/06_spain_text_pilot.py` identify exact Spanish word patterns for economy, institutions, climate/environment, identity and migration. Categories overlap. The economic/institutional indicator is the union of its two dictionaries. Counts are fractions of retained machine-defined spans containing an expression, not a fraction of all semantically relevant claims. The lexicon misses synonyms, implicit claims and meanings in context; inferences about the 80% proposition are not warranted.

For an error inspection, 12 matched and 12 unmatched spans were sampled within each party using random seed 42. The assistant conducting the analysis assigned provisional binary relevance labels with model results visible; this was not a blinded or independent human audit. For the economy/institution union, 20/24 matches were relevant and 9/24 non-matches were relevant. These intentionally stratified counts are not population accuracy or prevalence estimates, and only this union was audited. `spain_text_audit.csv` records the fixed sample identifiers, content hashes, model labels and provisional decisions without redistributing passages; the source text can be reconstructed locally to inspect individual errors. Examples of errors: employment surveys can match “empleo” without making an employment policy claim; discussion of labour reform or administrative procedures can be missed when dictionary terms are absent.

## 7. US newspaper index versus public issue mentions

`analysis/07_us_media_public.py` compares matching US Policy Agendas major codes for 2010–2016. Gallup's annual MIP data average poll-level issue shares after normalising all reported problem mentions to 100% within each poll. They are shares of mentions, not population proportions. NYT values are shares of the first index entry on odd-numbered pages, with a denominator of all entries with nonmissing major topic. The sample is not a news feed or audience exposure; NYT categories include foreign and local coverage. Although the topic scheme is aligned, coding and underlying units are not interchangeable. Differences do not identify media bias, causal influence or represent the original author's feed. Civil rights is broader than gender or LGBTQ topics. Data and [Gallup codebook](https://minio.la.utexas.edu/compagendas/codebookfiles/Gallups_Most_Important_Problem_Codebook.pdf) are available from [Policy Agendas](https://www.comparativeagendas.net/project/us/datasets).

## 8. Follow-up: Brazil, national priorities and category sensitivity

See [analysis/08_opinion_notes.md](analysis/08_opinion_notes.md) for exact question wording, fieldwork, published crosswalk, source access and licences. `08_brazil_and_six_country_opinion.py` processes ten independent Latinobarómetro waves, retaining all respondents in the primary denominator and exporting the alternative valid-response denominator and both narrow and broad economy definitions. Brazil's sample uses quota sampling at the last stage; the Wilson and endpoint intervals in the outputs are illustrative independent-binomial approximations, not design-based confidence intervals. A small point estimate or an interval crossing zero must not be interpreted as equivalence or statistical evidence of no change. Coding systems changed in 2018 and 2023; the 2010–2011 employment/immigration combined code is separated as ambiguous and the later pollution code is treated as a sensitivity category. The same script extracts SPES country-year values for Serbia, Spain and Bulgaria. These data do not supply design-based uncertainty, and the top-two prompted series cannot be pooled with Brazil's top-one open question.

## 9. Manifesto longitudinal and external-benchmark sensitivity

See [analysis/09_manifesto_notes.md](analysis/09_manifesto_notes.md) for codebook definitions, election coverage and document provenance. `09b_manifesto_longitudinal.py` produces election-level vote-weighted shares for economy 401–415, favourable environment 501, favourable disadvantaged minority groups 705, and multiculturalism 607–608 in five countries. Thailand and recent elections are absent; individual categories do not measure the complete issue agendas. The fixed-party comparison holds composition and weights constant but can cover only a small portion of votes, and coding-manual changes remain. `09_manifesto_benchmark.py` evaluates the prior Spanish dictionary against externally coded CAP quasi-sentences from two older documents. Their reference coding is independent of our dictionary, but category definitions and text segmentation differ. The benchmark is not a human peer review of this project or a validation of the 2023 data.

`analysis/90_claim_audit.py` calculates bounds assigning missing vote-weight the extreme category shares of 0% or 100%. These bounds are conditional on non-overlapping valid vote coverage and meaningful documents for unobserved parties. They are not confidence intervals or an estimate of the author's unspecified campaign-level 80%.

## 10. Spanish front pages and climate-news endpoint sensitivity

`10_spanish_frontpages.py` analyses CAP-coded front-page records for El País and El Mundo, separately, 2000–2011. The denominator is all observed, coded front-page records per outlet-year, including non-policy topics. Primary-topic codes 1, 5 and 15 form macroeconomics, labour and domestic commerce; code 2 covers broad civil rights, code 7 broad environment, and code 8 energy. Civil rights include topics well beyond gender identity; environment includes more than climate. No recent 2012–2026 content is present, and no inference should join these values to MeCCO counts.

We resample observed publication days, and as a sensitivity check observed calendar months, within outlet-year (2,000 replications, fixed seed). The monthly bootstrap is shown on the graph and has only 12 units per year. These intervals describe sensitivity to the temporal composition of observed front-page records under the resampling model; they do not account for coding, missing publications, serial dependence beyond the resampling unit, outlet selection or the Spanish media universe. The category-difference table uses a paired day bootstrap to preserve day-level co-occurrence. These are not estimates of audience exposure or an individual's feed.

`12_mecco_sensitivity.py` compares the same five fixed newspaper baskets at three different starting years and performs leave-one-newspaper-out checks for multisource baskets. It uses committed annual MeCCO count aggregates. No denominator of all publication output is available. Changes in archiving, outlet output, coverage and keyword queries remain plausible alternatives to changes in issue salience; counts do not establish shares.

## 11. ESS routed missing responses

`11_ess_routing.py` detects `ccnthum=55` together with missing `wrclmch`, recalculates valid-answer climate-worry shares, and assigns these routed cases either all to not strongly worried or all to strongly worried. Some self-completion respondents who answered the worry question despite `ccnthum=55` retain their observed answer. Per-wave extremes and independent-wave extreme bounds are measurement-sensitivity scenarios, not sampling confidence intervals. They exclude other kinds of missingness and cannot eliminate mode, design or time effects. All 44 original country-wave values and counts were separately checked against the baseline. Full source and remaining assumptions appear in [research_audit.md](research_audit.md).

## 12. Country structure, macroeconomic conditions and trust

`analysis/16_macro_conditions.py` merges the existing SPES survey data with World Bank WDI consumer inflation (`FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG`) and ILO-modelled unemployment (`SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS`) for 14 Western European countries, Spain, Bulgaria and Serbia. The regression sample is the original fixed set of 14 countries and 196 observed country-years in 2010–2024, with no 2011 SPES observation. Weighted-mean survey percentages and economic indicators have different denominators. Both issue equations are descriptive OLS with country-clustered standard errors and t inference, at most 14 clusters. Country and year indicators absorb persistent country differences and common annual variation, but cannot remove country-specific confounding, timing differences, changes in questions or reverse attribution. Outputs compare country-only and country-and-year indicators, leave-one-country-out estimates, the 2012–2019 period and exclusion of 2020–2021 or 2022–2023. These exploratory choices were not preregistered and should not be interpreted as a causal design.

Eurostat's annual `nama_10_lp_ulc` (`D1_SAL_PER`, current EUR) is compensation per employee, including employer social contributions. We divide it by annual all-items HICP (`prc_hicp_aind`, `INX_A_AVG`, `CP00`) and index the result to 2019=100. This is an average national-accounts compensation measure, not take-home income, median earnings or hourly wages, and is sensitive to the composition of employment. It is not interchangeable with the OECD's quarterly real hourly wage series. API snapshots and processed values preserve retrieval and calculation; exact hashes are listed in `data/raw/README.md`.

`analysis/17_insecurity_trust.py` reads ESS8/ESS10/ESS10SC. It groups `hincfel` responses 3–4 (difficult/very difficult) against 1–2 (comfortable/coping), and separately estimates trust in parliament `trstprl`, trust in politicians `trstplt`, cultural effect of immigration `imueclt`, and economic effect `imbgeco`. Each outcome is coded 0–10, with larger trust or more positive immigration assessments at 10. Missing or non-substantive values are excluded. Country/wave poststratification weights are normalised to 1; weighted linear models include country, wave, age, recorded gender and educational categories. Sensitivity adds self-placed ideology, country-wave interactions and excludes countries using round-10 self-completion. Country-clustered t intervals use 22 clusters (16 in the interview-only sample); they do not incorporate full survey stratification, PSUs or all mode effects. Income hardship and trust/attitudes are measured simultaneously and from independent cross-sections: reverse causation, common factors, response style and selection remain. Immigration attitudes are not immigration salience or voting behaviour. The geographic coverage overlaps only Spain and Israel among the six focus countries.

`analysis/18_structural_exposure.py` retrieves WDI series for the six countries and France. Net energy imports as a percentage of energy use are a physical energy-balance ratio, negative for net exporters; the indicator does not identify energy prices, exposure to a specific supplier, fiscal rents or who bears the shock. Trade is gross imports plus exports relative to GDP and can exceed 100%. International travel receipts divided by current GDP are gross foreign-currency receipts relative to GDP, not tourism value added or tourism's share of GDP. Central-government revenues excluding grants as a percentage of GDP are not general-government capacity or fiscal space; countries differ in decentralisation, debt, social-insurance systems and extra-budgetary revenue. Missing WDI observations remain missing, and country-level exposure does not establish the distribution across households.

## 13. Thai surveys, campaign and election evidence; Spanish CIS

`analysis/13_thailand_notes.md` documents Asia Foundation's consistent-geography 2009–2010 political-conflict question and conflicting figures in the original report for the economy in 2010. Its published percentages are transcribed by the script, not independently human-validated by this project. Thammasat 2021/2023 and Gallup 2025 are separately documented snapshots, not a combined trend. 2023–2026 party-list vote arithmetic uses a common party-choice denominator; political successors are not the same legal party and vote totals do not identify motivations or issue attention. Specific Move Forward and Pheu Thai campaign materials establish proposals, not the distribution of campaign coverage.

`analysis/14_identity_spain_cis.py` extracts percentages from eight primary CIS barometer PDFs, checks selected values, and records page, question, response label and SHA-256. The unweighted n, survey dates and modes are transcribed from source documentation rather than automatically verified. Up to three national problems can be spontaneously named; immigration, gender violence and climate are different categories, not a comprehensive identity index. Earlier interviews were in person and later interviews telephone; the gender violence category label and questionnaire order changed, and the 2025–2026 tables are labelled recoded. Missing separate climate categories in earlier sources remain missing. No survey-design variance is reconstructed from these aggregate tables. Brazil election studies cited in `analysis/14_identity_notes.md` offer different observational findings about cultural attitudes and voting under different samples and models; this project did not reanalyse those studies.
