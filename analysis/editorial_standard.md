# Editorial standard for the public article

The article is a narrative explanation. Its reader should be able to follow each inference using information already supplied in the text. Context may accumulate across sections; an isolated paragraph need not repeat every definition.

Check each section in this order:

1. **Context before result.** Before a percentage appears, identify the question, population, period, answer format and denominator. A chart caption may carry survey design details once the prose has introduced the measure.
2. **Define before using.** At first substantive mention, explain the role of a survey, institution, abbreviation or technical term. Later mentions can use the short name.
3. **Follow the argument.** Every paragraph should explain, qualify or extend a claim the reader has already encountered. A transition must name the actual connection, not promise a “turn” or “revelation.”
4. **Make numbers interpretable.** Give units, place, date and comparison base. Distinguish percent from percentage points and survey responses from economic rates.
5. **Match claim to evidence.** Repeated cross-sections do not track individuals. Co-movement does not identify a cause. Different survey questions cannot be joined into one series.
6. **Motivate every inclusion.** A country, source or historical episode belongs only if it tests or explains a specific part of the argument.
7. **Keep useful density.** Each sentence should provide a fact, mechanism, inference, qualification needed for that inference, or a clear transition.

Read the Russian and English versions independently. For each paragraph, ask what a new reader must infer without being told. Supply missing premises in the earliest place where they are needed. Then read it aloud for rhythm and remove repeated caveats. This semantic check is editorial, not a claim that automated tests can establish clarity.
