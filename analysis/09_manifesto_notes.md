# Manifesto analysis: sources, comparisons and stress tests

## Scope and reproducibility

`09b_manifesto_longitudinal.py` reads `data/raw/manifesto_2023a.csv` (SHA-256 `0e5b5d7eebbda9a81519f316333dcf8a3960006dcbb8149f82e271e142599670`) and generates election-level summaries for Brazil, Bulgaria, Israel, Serbia and Spain. Thailand is not present. Obtain the source from the Manifesto Project subject to its [terms](https://manifesto-project.wzb.eu/information/documents/terms_of_use); the current [2026a release](https://manifestoproject.wzb.eu/datasets/MPDS2026a) requires registration and was not available to this analysis. Citation: Lehmann et al. (2023), Manifesto Project Main Dataset 2023a, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.25522/manifesto.mpds.2023a); [2023a codebook](https://manifesto-project.wzb.eu/down/data/2023a/codebooks/codebook_MPDataset_MPDS2023a.pdf).

Each election summary is a vote-weighted average of available documents with positive vote share, positive coded-unit count and nonmissing categories. Brazil uses the first-round presidential vote, not legislative vote share. The vote coverage is the sum represented in available documents. Documents include party manifestos, coalition programmes and substitutes, so the single-party-only average and corresponding vote coverage are shown separately. These quantities are descriptive of available texts; no sampling confidence interval can capture missing parties or incorrect coding.

Four pre-specified source categories are shown separately: economy categories 401–415; positive environmental protection 501; favourable general references to disadvantaged minority groups 705; and positive or negative multiculturalism 607–608. Category 416, which can include environmentally motivated anti-growth or sustainability, is deliberately omitted from the economy category as a sensitivity check against the older 401–416 definition. These are category shares of coded quasi-sentences, not proportions of speakers, voters or campaign ads. None represents all economic issues, all climate positions or all identity topics. Specifically, 501 is positive environmental protection rather than all climate/energy content; 705 is only favourable *general* references and includes categories such as people with disabilities or immigrants, not just sexual minorities. Opposing positions and relevant passages coded elsewhere are omitted. Multiculturalism is also a specific issue, not all identity politics. Changes in these four category shares cannot establish a general economic-versus-identity shift.

The matched-party endpoint sensitivity uses parties with a single-party document at both endpoints, equal party weights within the two endpoints constructed from average vote shares across the endpoints. This holds party composition constant but yields only partial coverage. Brazil is excluded because the presidential candidate can change within the same party. Israel has no usable matching single-party documents at both endpoints; in Serbia the single matched party has under 2% of votes. Even within matched parties, content, coders, election contexts and manifesto length change. The source's own `manual` column indicates a change in coding instructions from version 4 to 5 between the selected endpoints in Bulgaria, Serbia and Spain; all six matched Spanish party IDs cross the version change. We have not isolated its effects from actual differences in text. These comparisons are descriptive sensitivity checks and should not be marketed as reliable temporal changes.

## External comparison of the Spanish dictionary

`09_manifesto_benchmark.py` independently checks the *existing* frozen Spanish dictionaries against externally classified Spanish quasi-sentence data. The reference is the Spanish Comparative Agendas Project's collection of governing/opposition party manifestos, coded by a separate research group. Chaqués Bonafont, Laura, Anna Palau and Frank R. Baumgartner (2015), *Agenda Dynamics in Spain*. [Source and dataset page](https://www.comparativeagendas.net/project/spain/datasets); [data](https://minio.la.utexas.edu/compagendas/datasetfiles/Party_Manifestos_CAP_Web_csv.csv); [codebook](https://minio.la.utexas.edu/compagendas/codebookfiles/Description_Party_Manifestos.pdf). Save the CSV as `data/raw/cap_spain_manifestos.csv`, SHA-256 `25404b088e8b49794e6a41d667d4295cffc46a4bb652c40a8c9899f4704875e7`. The raw text is omitted from this repository.

The benchmark uses all 2,252 coded PP 2000 quasi-sentences and 3,636 coded PSOE 2008 quasi-sentences with nonmissing text, year and topic. The older years are out of corpus relative to the 2023 programmes. CAP assigns one primary topic per quasi-sentence. We test the keyword union economy/institutions against the union of CAP 1 (macroeconomics), 5 (labour), 13 (social welfare), 15 (domestic commerce) and 20 (government operations). This reference is broader than the keyword list, while the keyword list can also match text with a different CAP primary category. Consequently the nominal FP/FN cells contain genuine dictionary errors *and* valid differences in coding scheme; they must not be described as independent human adjudication of our concept. The PP 2000 union comparison has 236 TP, 113 FP, 606 FN and 1,297 TN. PSOE 2008 has 306 TP, 222 FP, 950 FN and 2,158 TN. The precision/recall point estimates and intervals are in `data/processed/spain_cap_dictionary_benchmark.csv`.

The 95% intervals resample contiguous approximately 30-unit blocks from the same document 1,500 times. They indicate sensitivity to local text composition under this resampling scheme, not design-based or population confidence intervals, and do not validate the punctuation-delimited 2023 corpus, future elections or other countries. Rows with no positive prediction/reference in a resample are omitted for precision/recall respectively; such intervals are unstable for rare topics. The resulting low recall for the broad economy/institutions comparison warns against treating lexical matches as agenda shares. The identity lexicon is intentionally narrower than all CAP civil rights, so its low recall does not measure accuracy for LGBTQ-specific references. The environment dictionary includes renewable-energy wording that can fall outside CAP environmental protection. Differences in unit segmentation, coding periods and source material limit transportability.

`spain_cap_disagreement_ids.csv` records deterministic samples of up to eight alleged false positives and eight alleged false negatives per topic and document. It contains source IDs, hash and reference topic but no source passage. To inspect the texts, match `cap_id` to `id` in the downloaded CSV. “False positive/negative” denotes disagreement with the broad CAP mapping, not a verified coding error. No claim of a new independent human or blinded peer review is made.
